## The problem is not only technical

A production bug has a visible cost: incident response, hotfix, communication and sometimes a commercial gesture. A flaky test is quieter. It passes on Monday, fails on Tuesday, passes again after a rerun and leaves the team with a toxic question: should we trust CI?

When that question takes root, the test suite stops acting as a safety net. Developers rerun jobs, reviewers ignore signals and product managers see releases slip without a clear reason.

## The real cost of instability

The first cost is human time. Ten minutes lost per pipeline sounds acceptable until it is multiplied by pull requests, developers and weeks of delivery.

The second cost is trust. A reliable failing test triggers investigation. A flaky failing test triggers a rerun. That difference turns quality signal into administrative noise.

The third cost is product risk. The more a team ignores tests, the more likely it becomes to ship a real regression.

## Stabilize without throwing everything away

Start by ranking the failures. Which tests fail most often? On which browsers? After which changes? At which CI step? Without this triage, the team fixes symptoms at random.

Then isolate critical journeys. Login, checkout, payment, billing or account creation do not carry the same risk as a secondary filter. Treat the critical paths like product infrastructure.

Finally, repair the foundations: stable selectors, clear fixtures, isolated data, controlled network dependencies, limited retries and useful reporting.

## What to measure

Track rerun frequency, diagnosis time, the most unstable tests, pipeline duration and regressions caught before production. These indicators translate QA work into business language.

Stable tests are not a technical luxury. They reduce release risk and protect delivery focus.
