## AI accelerates, but it does not replace QA judgment

Generative AI is useful for drafting test scenarios, reformulating cases, analyzing logs, proposing assertions or creating a first Playwright skeleton. It saves time when the need is clearly framed.

The trap is confusing volume with coverage. Twenty generated tests have little value if they miss the important risks, use uncontrolled data or assert only that a page does not crash.

## The illusion of coverage

A suite created too quickly can look reassuring: many files, many steps, many green reports. But meaningful coverage starts from business journeys, not the DOM.

Checking that a button exists can be useful. Proving that the right user can complete an order, receive confirmation and find it in their account is far more important.

AI does not automatically know your business model, sensitive customers, past incidents or product trade-offs. Without that context, it optimizes syntax, not risk.

## The useful pattern

Use AI as an analysis accelerator. Give it an incident, CI logs and an unstable test: it can suggest hypotheses. Give it a well-written user story: it can list edge cases. Give it your test conventions: it can draft coherent scenarios.

But senior QA must keep the decision: which journeys deserve automation, which assertions prove value, which tests block release and which checks should remain manual.

AI is powerful when it serves a strategy. It becomes dangerous when it replaces one.
