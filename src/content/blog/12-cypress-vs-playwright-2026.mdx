## The right tool depends on context

The Cypress versus Playwright question comes up often, but the useful answer rarely starts with the tools themselves. Both can produce excellent E2E suites. The real criteria are your product risks, team skills, frontend architecture, target browsers, CI speed and debugging needs.

For a startup, the best choice is not always the most complete tool. It is the tool the team can still maintain six months later, after the product has changed several times.

## When Cypress still makes sense

Cypress keeps a very strong developer experience. Its interactive runner, browser-focused mental model and ecosystem make it a good fit for frontend teams that need fast local diagnosis.

It is especially relevant for apps where local debugging matters, teams already comfortable with JavaScript and suites mostly targeting Chromium.

## When Playwright has the edge

Playwright shines when multi-browser coverage matters, when tests must run quickly in parallel or when the team needs detailed browser-context control. Chromium, Firefox and WebKit support is a concrete advantage for B2B products used in varied environments.

Its traces, videos and snapshots also make CI diagnosis easier without reproducing every failure locally.

## A practical recommendation

Start from risk. If your product is an internal controlled application with one target browser, Cypress may be enough. If your product is a public SaaS with enterprise clients, multi-browser expectations and demanding pipelines, Playwright often becomes the more natural fit.

Do not migrate just because a benchmark looks attractive. Measure on two or three critical journeys and compare writing time, diagnosis time, CI stability and how easily the team can own the suite.

The tool is only one part of success. Quality comes from test strategy, controlled data, useful assertions and a CI pipeline that gives trustworthy signals.
